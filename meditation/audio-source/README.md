# Sound sources

The four WAVs are the original Meditation 1.0.4 synthesized presets, retained for rebuilding. `zen_tone_original.mp3` is the user-supplied `alex_jauk-zen-tone-mid-high-202557.mp3` (Alex Jauk, as credited in the supplied filename).

`../tools/extend-sounds.py` preserves the initial half-second strike, stretches the ringing tail without shifting pitch, crossfades the join and applies a raised-cosine fade from 2 to 19.8 seconds followed by 0.2 seconds of silence. A limiter catches processing peaks. Outputs are Ogg Vorbis; Zen tone keeps the original recording’s stereo channels. These source files are not packaged in the app. `processing.json` records source/output hashes and processing parameters.

Zen tone and Singing bowl now keep their real-time initial strikes; the whole sound is never stretched or faded in slowly. Zen tone preserves the original recording through 1.4 seconds, silences only its low-level pre-strike lead-in, and uses a 10ms entry fade. Its late ringing modes are fitted from the original recording and extended with their phases retained, avoiding a phase-vocoder attack. Singing bowl keeps its first 1.2 seconds, adds a short deterministic struck-mallet transient made from damped inharmonic modes (no noise), then joins a phase-aligned extended ring. The two outputs use lossless stereo/mono FLAC. The other three Ogg presets remain unchanged.

Both sounds last twenty seconds and fade smoothly to silence by 19.8 seconds. `audio-check.py` compares their initial waveform to the original source, so a whole-file stretch or a long fade-in fails even if it is click-free. Source attack preservation, clipping, sample steps, duration and final silence are checked. The bowl’s tail join is phase-aligned by normalized correlation; its measured correlation is recorded in `processing.json`.

Rebuild either with `python3 tools/extend-sounds.py --only zen_tone` or `--only warm_bowl`. These two rebuilds additionally require NumPy and SciPy (`python3 -m pip install numpy scipy`); those packages are development-only and are not used in the app or by the verification script. Android resources remain uncompressed for seekable playback.

Brown noise, white noise and rain are original synthesized, periodic 24-second background loops. Their sources contain no recordings. Rebuild with tools/make-ambience.py (NumPy and FFmpeg); ambient-processing.json records the generated hashes and seed. tools/ambient-check.py verifies their duration, audible level, headroom, continuity at the loop boundary and lack of silent chunks. These loops are played by a separate looping player and do not change the five bell presets.
